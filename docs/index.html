The method on one page

How the model turns ball-by-ball data into two top 10s

1

The data

883 matches, ball by ball. 735 rated: IPL 2022–26 and full-member T20Is.

→

2

Win chance

A model gives the batting side’s chance of winning before every ball.

→

3

Pressure Index

How far a ball can swing that chance, ranked 0 to 100. Top 20% = pressure balls

→

4

Ball value

Runs above an average player in that exact spot. A wicket is worth 8 runs.

↓ then

5

Fair comparison

Adjusted for who they faced. Bowlers also for ground, dew and home side.

→

6

Pressure Rating

Every ball weighted by its Index, then small samples pulled toward average.

→

7

2,000 resamples

Redraw the matches 2,000 times and count each player’s top-10 finishes.

Two top 10s

The ten who make the top 10 most often, ordered by rating.

Then I tried to break it: ten changes to the settings, six more pressure ideas, and a test of 2022–24 ratings against 2025–26.

A pressure ball is one in the top 20% of the Pressure Index (80 or more), about 48 a match. 77% of them come in the chase, and 45% in the middle overs, not only at the death. Full detail, formulas and checks are in my methodology report.
